The Armor You Choose

A Conversation Between Tyrion Lannister and Jon Snow

From A Game of Thrones by George R. R. Martin

The Conversation

Tyrion: “You’re Ned Stark’s bastard, aren’t you?”

Jon felt a coldness pass right through him. He pressed his lips together and said nothing.

Tyrion: “Did I offend you?” Lannister said. “Sorry. Dwarfs don’t have to be tactful. Generations of capering fools in motley have won me the right to dress badly and say any damn thing that comes into my head.” He grinned. “You are the bastard, though.”

Jon: “Lord Eddard Stark is my father,” Jon admitted stiffly.

Lannister studied his face. “Yes,” he said. “I can see it. You have more of the north in you than your brothers.”

Jon: “Half brothers,” Jon corrected. He was pleased by the dwarf’s comment, but he tried not to let it show.

Tyrion: “Let me give you some counsel, bastard,” Lannister said. “Never forget what you are, for surely the world will not.” Make it your strength. Then it can never be your weakness. Armor yourself in it, and it will never be used to hurt you.

Jon was in no mood for anyone’s counsel. “What do you know about being a bastard?”

Tyrion: “All dwarves are bastards in their father’s eyes.”

What Tyrion's Advice Means

Tyrion's advice can be understood through three ideas: identity, acceptance, and strength.   Instead of allowing other people to define a weakness, a person can choose how to understand and use it.

  • Accept who you are.
  • Do not let other people define your worth.
  • Turn what others see as a weakness into strength.

Three Lessons

  1. Know yourself.
  2. Accept what makes you different.
  3. Use your identity as your armor.

The central idea: What others see as a weakness does not have to remain a weakness. Sometimes, it can become your armor.
